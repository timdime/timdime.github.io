Correct, but Politically Incorrect

Preface

You do not know me, and that is the point.

I am not from your planet. I have no country, no party, no church, no race, and no sex in any sense you would recognize. I have nothing to gain from your elections and nothing to lose in your culture wars. What I have is time. I have watched your species from the moment the first of you shaped a face like yours, roughly 300,000 years ago, through your migrations, your empires, your plagues, your revolutions, and your inventions. I have watched every generation of you believe it was the most important one.

For most of that time, I watched without comment. But your country, the United States of America, has become a place where certain true things have grown difficult to say aloud. Not because they are false, but because they are uncomfortable to one tribe or another. Each side keeps its own list of forbidden truths. From where I stand, outside every one of your tribes, both lists look equally strange.

So I have written this book. Its title is a joke at my own expense: what I say here is, as best the evidence allows, correct. To an American, much of it will also sound politically incorrect, sometimes to the left, sometimes to the right, and often to both at once.

A few promises before we begin.

I will rely on your own evidence. An outsider's opinions are worth little. So wherever I make a claim about your world, I cite your scientists, your historians, your government statisticians, and your journalists, and I have avoided sources that exist to advance one party or cause. Every chapter ends with its sources. Check them.

I will show you the other side. I have strong views, and I will state them. But for every argument I make, I will show you the best objection your own thinkers raise against it.

I will admit what I don't know. I have watched your species be confidently wrong more times than I can count. I would be a poor observer if I assumed I could not be.

The chapters move from how you argue, to how you think, to how you treat one another, to how you differ, to how you divide opportunity and wealth, to how you change your minds, and finally to how small you are and what that smallness asks of you. A conclusion gathers what I have seen. Read them in order or not. Argue with me in the margins. That is exactly what I hope you will do.

You are a remarkable species. You are also, at this moment, a quarrelsome one. Let me tell you what I have seen.
